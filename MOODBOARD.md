## What "Monochromatic" Means for This Project

It does NOT mean boring.

It means:
- **Dark mode:** Near-black background, white text, hairline borders. The only "color" is the subtle brightness difference between elements.
- **Light mode:** Off-white background, near-black text, light grey borders.
- **Interest comes from:** Size contrast, weight contrast, spacing, alignment — not hue.

The effect: the portfolio looks as clean and considered as an Apple product page or a museum exhibition label.

## The Award as a Quiet Statement

The WorldSkills Medal of Excellence is a remarkable achievement.

Most portfolios would put this front and center with a gold badge, sparkle animation, and amber glow.

This portfolio buries it in the factoid list in the About section AND gives it a clean bordered callout in the Education section — with NO glow, NO animation, NO emoji (except maybe the 🏅 icon).

**Why:** Confidence doesn't announce itself. It lets the achievement speak.

## The Secret Sauce: The Detail Layer

What separates a 7/10 minimalist portfolio from a 10/10 one is the **detail layer** — the things most visitors never consciously notice, but which make the experience feel premium.

For this portfolio:

1. **Section index numbers** — `[01]`, `[02]`, `[03]` in mono tertiary — editorial, newspaper-inspired
2. **Hairline horizontal rules** — 1px, barely visible, structuring the page mathematically
3. **Em dashes over hyphens** — in job titles and descriptions
4. **Dot-separated tech stacks** — `python · fastapi · postgresql` — not comma-separated, not tag-only
5. **No widows in text** — max 65ch line length prevents orphaned words
6. **Consistent arrow direction** — external links always `↗`, scroll-down always `↓`, top always `↑`
7. **The tagline in PT by default** — shows Daniel is proudly Portuguese, not trying to be generic
8. **"Open to work" in the factoid list** — not buried, not a banner — just a fact, cleanly stated

These details are what make someone say: **"wait, how is this portfolio SO good?"**

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid (Lessons from 10 Years of Portfolio Reviews)

| Pattern | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Skill progress bars (`Python: 85%`) | Meaningless. Who measures skill in %? It screams junior. |
| "Hello, I'm [name]" hero text | Every portfolio does this. It's dead. |
| Bright colored accent on everything | Color says "I know CSS". Restraint says "I know design." |
| Animated background on hero | Competes with the content. Visitors look at the animation, not you. |
| "I'm passionate about coding" | Generic. Replace with specific, true things. |
| Circular progress indicators | See skill bars. Same problem. |
| Full-page contact form | Friction. Email link is enough. |
| "Hover to see more" hidden content | Mobile users can't hover. Content should be visible. |
| 3 fonts + 3 accent colors | Visual noise. |
| Projects with no context | "I built an app" — so what? Show the decision, the challenge, the result. |
